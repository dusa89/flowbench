Playbook #1: The 4-Step Missed-Call Recovery System for Home-Service Contractors (2026)

The short version

A home-service contractor's phone rings while the tech is in an attic — and voicemail loses the job to the competitor who picked up. This playbook wires four automations together: (1) instant missed-call text-back, (2) an AI voice agent that answers after hours and books into your calendar, (3) automatic appointment confirmations and reminders that cut no-shows, and (4) automatic review requests after every finished job. The back end is Make (visual automation, start with the free 1,000 credits/mo tier) plus Synthflow for the voice agent; ElevenLabs voices make the agent sound human. You can buy this as a turnkey product instead — vendors selling ready-made versions run roughly $59–$199/mo (reported by ai.advalorem.io, October 2026) — but the DIY route below runs on the same tools for less and you keep the customer data.

Step 1 — Catch every missed call in under 60 seconds

1The missed-call text-back

When a call goes unanswered, the automation texts the caller immediately: "Sorry we missed you — what's the job? We'll reply in minutes." For a plumber, this is the whole game: an emergency customer dials the next number on Google in under a minute. Speed of response is the close.

Built with: Make scenario — missed-call trigger → send SMS → log the lead. This is the easiest first automation and the highest-ROI one. Compare: why Make over n8n for this playbook's back end (free 1,000 credits/mo, gentlest learning curve for a non-technical owner).

Step 2 — Let an AI voice agent answer after hours

2The after-hours voice agent

A voice agent answers the overflow and after-hours calls: it greets the caller, captures name, address, job type and urgency, checks your real calendar availability, and books the appointment. It handles the morning-rush stack of three calls at once — no second phone needed.

Built with: Synthflow (voice agent, books into your calendar; agency tier exists for agencies reselling to contractors — Synthflow's own materials, October 2026) with an ElevenLabs voice under the hood for natural speech. One honest caveat: ElevenLabs affiliate terms pay 22% of payments for the first 12 months on Starter/Creator/Pro/Scale tiers, 11% on Business (elevenlabs.io/affiliates-terms, "Last Updated: 10 June 2026") — no earnings cap, but cookie duration and payout minimum are not published on the public page.

Step 3 — Kill no-shows with automatic confirmations and reminders

3The confirmation + reminder chain

The moment a job books — by the voice agent or by you — the automation sends an instant confirmation, then a reminder 24 hours before and again 2 hours before the appointment. Every reminder carries a one-tap "running late / reschedule" path back into the calendar. This is pure calendar → SMS plumbing; no AI needed.

Built with: Make — calendar event → timed SMS sends → reschedule webhook. If a customer replies "can't make it," route it back to the voice agent (Step 2) to rebook instead of to your pocket.

Step 4 — Turn every finished job into a Google review

4The automatic review request

After the invoice is paid or the job is marked complete, the automation texts the customer a direct Google review link. Home-service businesses live on reviews — this one converts jobs you already finished into the star rating that wins the next job.

Built with: Make — job-complete trigger → delayed SMS with your review link. Send it within a day of the finished job; waiting longer measurably shrinks response rates.

Common objections, answered honestly

Will customers know it's AI?

Some will, some won't. What matters is it picks up, gets the details right, and books the job — a voice agent that answers in one ring beats a human who answers tomorrow. Disclose it as an assistant; pretending otherwise backfires.

I'm not technical — can I actually set this up?

Step 1 and Step 3 are point-and-click Make scenarios. Step 2 (the voice agent) is the hard one; Synthflow's no-code builder is made for non-developers and agencies resell it to contractors who never touch the dashboard themselves. Start with Step 1 alone — it pays for everything else.

What about outbound calls and compliance?

This playbook is inbound-first on purpose. Automated outbound calling has federal and state rules (and carrier rules) that change often — check the current rules for your state before automating any outbound calls. Nothing here requires outbound.
