The hard constraint: HIPAA comes before any of the steps below

Dental offices handle protected health information — appointment details, insurance IDs, treatment plans. Any voice agent, SMS tool, or automation that touches patient data needs a signed Business Associate Agreement (BAA) with each component that stores or transmits it. This is the fork the whole market splits on: Trillet sells a $49/mo plan (150 minutes included, $0.20/min overage, per its own comparison page, late September 2026) that is explicitly non-PHI, direct-to-consumer — cheap, but the compliance surface is yours to manage — while Voicify (founded 2017, dedicated dental product) claims HIPAA support and keeps pricing quote-gated. The DIY route below is only viable with BAA-signed components; a practice that will not sign BAAs should buy turnkey from a vendor that will put its HIPAA claims in the contract. Nothing below is compliance advice — verify with each vendor and your counsel.

The short version

Dental practices lose an estimated 30% of new-patient calls during business hours (Logic Impact AI, May 2026; offices reportedly miss 20–30% of calls during lunch and busy periods), and the average new patient's lifetime value is reported at $2,500 (third-party claim — attribute it, don't bank it). This playbook wires five automations together: (1) instant missed-call text-back, (2) an AI receptionist that answers overflow and after-hours calls and books into your schedule, (3) automatic insurance verification before the visit, (4) confirmation and reminder chains that cut no-shows, and (5) hygiene recall and reactivation campaigns that refill the schedule from patients you already have. The back end is Make (visual automation, free 1,000 credits/mo to start) plus Synthflow for the voice agent; ElevenLabs voices make the agent sound human. Every step sits behind the HIPAA/BAA gate in the box above — read that first.

Step 1 — Catch every missed call in under 60 seconds

1The missed-call text-back

When a call goes unanswered — lunch rush, hygienist on the phone, after hours — the automation texts the caller immediately: "Sorry we missed you — new patient? Reply with what you need and we'll get you scheduled." A new patient who hears voicemail calls the next practice on Google. Speed of response is the close.

Built with: Make scenario — missed-call trigger → SMS → lead logged with call time and number. Easiest first automation, highest ROI. Compare: why Make over n8n for this playbook's back end (free 1,000 credits/mo, gentlest learning curve for a non-technical office manager). See the same step for contractors: playbook #1.

Step 2 — Let an AI receptionist answer overflow and after hours

2The HIPAA-aware voice agent

A voice agent answers the calls the front desk can't: it greets the caller, distinguishes new patients from existing ones, captures chief complaint and availability, and books into your real schedule — including the after-hours calls that otherwise go to voicemail. The dental-specific skills that matter: confirming the patient is established or new, collecting insurance carrier and member ID for the verification step below, and routing genuine emergencies (trauma, swelling, severe pain) to the on-call protocol instead of a booking link.

Built with: Synthflow (voice agent, books into your calendar; agency tier exists for agencies reselling to practices — Synthflow's own materials, October 2026) with an ElevenLabs voice under the hood. BAA first: confirm in writing which components sign a BAA before any patient data flows through them — this is the difference between the $49/mo non-PHI tools and the quote-gated HIPAA-claiming ones (Trillet's own comparison, late September 2026; Voicify's HIPAA claim). One honest affiliate caveat: ElevenLabs pays 22% of payments for the first 12 months on Starter/Creator/Pro/Scale tiers, 11% on Business (elevenlabs.io/affiliates-terms, "Last Updated: 10 June 2026") — no earnings cap, but cookie duration and payout minimum are not published on the public page.

Step 3 — Verify insurance before the visit, automatically

3The insurance-verification pass

New-patient no-shows and day-of billing surprises both start with unverified insurance. The automation takes the carrier and member ID captured in Step 2, runs the eligibility check ahead of the appointment (third-party dental front-desk roundups describe verification tools covering hundreds of payers — e.g. SavvyAgents, updated ~2 months ago), and flags ineligible or inactive plans to the front desk before the patient is in the chair — not after. The cost estimate the patient heard on the phone then matches the bill, which is what gets the five-star review.

Built with: Make scenario — new booking → pull insurance fields → eligibility check via your verification provider → flag or confirm back into the schedule. Wire this into your practice management system (Dentrix, Eaglesoft, Open Dental are the common ones) so eligibility lives on the patient's record, not in a spreadsheet.

Step 4 — Kill no-shows with confirmations and reminders

4The confirmation + reminder chain

The moment an appointment books — by the voice agent or the front desk — the automation sends an instant confirmation with the date, time, address, and parking note, then a reminder 48 hours before and again the morning of. Every reminder carries a one-tap "running late / reschedule" path. Dental no-shows are disproportionately expensive: a missed hygiene slot is an hour of hygienist payroll with zero production.

Built with: Make — calendar/schedule event → timed SMS sends → reschedule webhook. If a patient replies "can't make it," route it back to the voice agent (Step 2) to rebook instead of to the front desk's voicemail.

Step 5 — Refill the schedule from patients you already have

5Hygiene recall + reactivation campaigns

The cheapest new appointment is a lapsed one. The automation watches your schedule for patients overdue for their 6-month hygiene visit (or treatment-planned but unscheduled — ADA procedure codes on the treatment plan tell you exactly who these are) and sends a timed recall sequence: a friendly nudge, a value reminder ("your benefits may reset"), and a final reactivation offer. This is the step turnkey vendors price highest — third-party roundups list retention platforms at $300–$500/mo (Mediaffy, 2026) — because reactivated patients are pure margin against the $2,500 lifetime-value figure quoted above.

Built with: Make — scheduled scan of the PMS for overdue recall/treatment-plan patients → segmented SMS/email sequence → booking link. Keep the cadence conservative; patients who feel spammed leave one-star reviews that cost more than the recall earned.

Common objections, answered honestly

Is a DIY voice agent actually HIPAA-compliant?

It can be — if every component that touches patient data signs a BAA, and you verify it in writing. It is not compliant by default. This is the single biggest difference between this playbook and the contractors' one: a plumber's missed-call log is a phone number; a dental missed-call log is protected health information the moment it includes why the patient called. Do the BAA diligence first, build second. Nothing here is compliance advice.

Will it integrate with my practice management software?

Plan on Dentrix, Eaglesoft, or Open Dental as the system of record and check each integration before you commit — the automation is only as good as the schedule it reads. The Make back end connects through APIs and webhooks; the voice agent needs read/write access to real availability, not a static calendar.

I'm not technical — can my office manager set this up?

Steps 1, 4, and 5 are point-and-click Make scenarios. Step 2 (the voice agent) is the hard one; Synthflow's no-code builder is made for non-developers, and agencies resell it to practices that never touch the dashboard themselves. Start with Step 1 alone — at a 30% missed-call rate, it pays for everything else.

What about outbound calls and compliance?

This playbook is inbound-first on purpose. Automated outbound calling — and healthcare marketing messages — carry federal and state rules (plus carrier rules) that change often. Check the current rules for your state and your counsel's read before automating any outbound or marketing sends. Nothing here requires outbound.
